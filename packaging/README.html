Fortnite Visual Chest Counter

Automatically count Fortnite chests from gameplay video · v0.3.0 public beta · Windows 10 / 11 · no Python or programming needed

Start here

  1. Right-click the ZIP and choose Extract All. A normal folder such as Downloads or Desktop is fine, but not Program Files. Do not run the app from inside the ZIP.
  2. Open the extracted folder and double-click Fortnite Visual Chest Counter.exe. It is the only thing you open. Nothing else needs installing; you only need OBS if you want the numbers on your stream or want to count from your own OBS scene.
Important: always extract the complete archive. Do not delete files from the runtime folder or move individual files out of the app’s folder. Doing so may stop the app from starting or counting correctly. That folder holds the app’s own copy of Python, FFmpeg and its window toolkit: these are expected parts of the app.

Your data stays on your PC next to the app: state (counts, profiles, match history) and config.toml (settings, once you have changed one). To back up, close the app and copy those two. Deleting the app’s folder deletes them too. The built-in updater is experimental; see Updating.

What this is

A small app for players and streamers. It recognises the chest-opening pop-ups in Fortnite gameplay video and keeps a running count of the chests opened — from a Twitch or YouTube livestream link, anything shown on a screen, your own stream's OBS feed (one PC or two), a capture card or a recorded video. If you want the numbers on stream, it writes them into a text file that a normal OBS text source shows (you choose the font, size and colours in OBS). It does not touch Fortnite, the game files or your account: it only looks at the video picture, like a viewer would, and never shows anything that is not already on screen (see Fair play and compatibility).

CERTAIN OPENED CHESTS

Chests the counter is strongly confident about. A safe, conservative number.

APPROX. OPENED CHESTS

The estimated TOTAL of opened chests, including the certain ones. It also adds chests where the evidence was a bit weaker.
Important - the two numbers are NOT added together.
CERTAIN: 12 and APPROX: 47 means “about 47 chests in total, and 12 of those I am very sure about”. It does not mean 59. APPROX is always the same as or higher than CERTAIN.

This is a public beta. APPROX is an estimate, not a perfect count. In our recorded test streams it matched the real number closely, but computer vision is never 100%. See Known limitations.

Count from a Twitch or YouTube livestream URL

No OBS, capture card or browser needed. In the app: Video source → Livestream URL (Twitch / YouTube), paste the link (https://www.twitch.tv/CHANNEL or a YouTube watch?v= / @CHANNEL/live link), keep Stream stability: Maximum stability (recommended), click Start counting. The stream must be live and public. Tested for hours at a time (a 3-hour session counted about 500 chests; that count was not checked chest by chest).

This is the convenient option, not the most accurate one. Treat its number as a lower-confidence estimate: chests can be missed, sometimes with nothing on screen to tell you so. How many has not been measured. To count your own play as reliably as possible, use OBS Virtual Camera or a capture card.
  • The counter reads the stream as viewers get it, a few seconds to about a minute behind. It checks every picture that reaches it, in order, but it can only count what arrives: stream compression can make a pop-up unreadable, and video lost to ad breaks, buffering, reconnects or the counter falling behind is never seen.
  • Stream stability: Maximum stability = lowest buffering risk, most delay (best for long sessions); Balanced = moderate buffering risk; Low latency = higher buffering risk, not recommended when complete counts matter. No setting can bring back video the platform never delivered.
  • Good to know: video can arrive in bursts or pause to buffer; ad breaks and stream restarts can make the counter miss chests. The app reconnects by itself, keeps your counts, and says so clearly (red Reconnecting in the status, the Activity log and over the Live preview), including how many seconds could not be seen.

Count what is on a screen

Play the stream, video or game fullscreen on one of your monitors, pick that Screen in the Video source list (it shows each screen’s size; Refresh after plugging one in) and click Start counting. Turn on Live preview (Dashboard, Game view) once to check that the counter sees the game with its HUD.

  • The gameplay must fill the screen. A small player inside a web page (with chat and menus around it) is too small to read — nothing is counted. Use the player’s fullscreen button.
  • Keep other windows off that screen while counting. A 16:9 screen works best.

What you need

  • Windows 10 or 11.
  • Fortnite shown in a 16:9 picture (1920×1080 is ideal; 1280×720 or higher works) with the HUD visible, in English or Brazilian Portuguese.
  • About half of one CPU core. No graphics card needed.
  • OBS Studio 28 or newer only if you count from your own OBS scene, or want the numbers on stream.
  • Nothing else to install. Everything the app needs is already inside the folder.

Your own stream with OBS: the 30-second version

  1. Unzip the folder anywhere you can write files (Desktop or Documents is fine).
  2. In OBS: make a scene called ChestDetectorFeed with only your game in it, and start the Virtual Camera from that scene.
  3. Open Fortnite Visual Chest Counter.exe, pick OBS Virtual Camera as the video source and click Start counting.
  4. In OBS, add a Text source that reads obs_output\chest_counter.txt. The app’s Overlay page shows the exact path (with a Copy button) and lets you choose what it says.

Details for each step are below.

Your own stream: single-PC setup (game and OBS on the same PC)

1. Unzip

Right-click the zip → Extract All. Do not run it from inside the zip, and don’t put it in Program Files. The folder contains:

Fortnite Visual Chest Counter.exe   <- the app: the only thing you open
README.html                    this guide
config.example.toml            optional advanced settings (see "Settings")
obs_output\                    the text files OBS reads
state\  logs\                  your counter and logs (kept when you update)
runtime\                       program files (the official Python runtime from python.org, FFmpeg, Qt) - don't touch

2. Make a clean feed for the counter in OBS

The counter must see only the game picture (no webcam, alerts or overlays covering the screen). The easiest way is a separate scene that you never show on stream.

  1. Open OBS. In the Scenes box click + and name the scene exactly ChestDetectorFeed.
  2. In the Sources box click + → Game Capture → OK. Set Mode to “Capture specific window” and choose Fortnite (start Fortnite first so it appears in the list). If Game Capture shows a black picture, use Display Capture instead.
  3. Make Fortnite fill the whole scene: select the source and press Ctrl+F (“Fit to screen”).
  4. Do not add a webcam, alerts, chat or the chest-counter text to this scene.

3. Start the Virtual Camera from that scene

  1. In OBS, find the Controls box (bottom-right). Click the small gear next to Start Virtual Camera.
  2. Set Output Type to Scene and Output Selection to ChestDetectorFeed. Close the window.
  3. Click Start Virtual Camera. It now shows as running.

Your normal stream scene is unaffected: viewers still see whatever scene you choose. The counter reads only ChestDetectorFeed.

4. Start counting

Open Fortnite Visual Chest Counter.exe. On the Dashboard, pick OBS Virtual Camera as the Video source; click Start counting. The status at the top right changes to Counting, and a line under the video source shows the picture size and how many pictures per second are analysed (about 15).

Leave the app open while you play; you can minimise it. Each chest appears in the Activity log (Dashboard, Console view) the moment it is counted and the big numbers go up. To stop, click Stop counting (or close the app; it asks first). Your numbers are saved after every chest and are kept for next time.

If a red message says “OBS Virtual Camera was not found”, follow the steps it shows (open OBS, Start Virtual Camera, click Start counting again).

5. Show the numbers on stream

See Show it on stream below.

Two-PC setup (gaming PC + streaming PC with a capture card)

Gaming PC / console ↓ HDMI Capture card ↓ Streaming PC → OBS → scene "ChestDetectorFeed" → Virtual Camera → Fortnite Visual Chest Counter
  • Unzip and open the app on the streaming PC (the one running OBS). Nothing needs to run or be installed on the gaming PC or console.
  • In OBS on the streaming PC, create the ChestDetectorFeed scene and add your capture card as a Video Capture Device source. Make it fill the scene (Ctrl+F). Nothing else goes in this scene.
  • Set the Virtual Camera to Scene → ChestDetectorFeed and click Start Virtual Camera (exact clicks in step 3 above).
  • In the app, pick OBS Virtual Camera as the video source and click Start counting — the same as on one PC.
Advanced: reading the capture card directly (no Virtual Camera). Your capture card also appears in the app’s Video source list (“Capture device: …”). Many capture cards can only be opened by one program at a time, so this does not work while OBS is using the card. If the app says the device cannot be opened, switch back to OBS Virtual Camera.

Other video inputs (Virtual Camera already in use?)

For OBS users whose Virtual Camera is already taken, or who want to read a capture card directly. These are marked less tested in the Video source list (click Refresh if you just plugged something in).

OBS Windowed Projector (capture card and Virtual Camera already in use)

If OBS owns the capture card and the Virtual Camera is used by another program, the counter can read your gameplay from an OBS projector window instead. The card is not opened a second time, no Virtual Camera or plugin is needed, and your stream stays exactly as it is.

  1. In OBS, right-click your gameplay source (for example the capture card’s Video Capture Device), in the Sources list or in the preview, and choose Windowed Projector (Source). A window opens showing only that source.
  2. Make that window at least 1280×720. Bigger is fine, and any shape works: black bars are cut off automatically.
  3. Recommended: OBS → Settings > General > Projectors → tick Save projectors on exit, so the projector reopens every time OBS starts.
  4. In the app click Refresh, then pick OBS projector: Windowed Projector (Source) - your source in the Video source list.
  5. Click Start counting. On the Dashboard’s Game view, turn on Live preview once to check that the whole picture is your gameplay with the HUD.
  • The projector may stay behind other windows, but it must not be minimized: a minimized window has no picture to count.
  • Closing the projector or OBS stops counting, with a message. Open it again and click Start counting.
  • The projector shows the source itself, not your live scene, so switching scenes or overlays on your stream does not change what the counter sees.
  • If you rename the gameplay source, its projector gets a new title: pick it again in the list.
Less tested: this has been tested with a stand-in window, and not yet with a real OBS projector and a physical capture card.

Show it on stream (OBS text source)

The counter only writes the text. You style it in OBS however you like. You need one source:

  1. Open the scene your viewers see (not ChestDetectorFeed).
  2. In Sources click + → Text (GDI+) → name it “Chests” → OK.
  3. Tick Read from file, click Browse and choose obs_output\chest_counter.txt (inside the counter folder). The app’s Overlay page shows this exact path; Copy path puts it on the clipboard.
  4. Choose a font, size and colour. Click OK and place it on screen.
Put the text source only in the scene your viewers see, never in ChestDetectorFeed.

This needs the app and OBS on the same PC, because OBS reads the file from the counter’s folder. The file is rewritten the moment a chest is counted, and OBS re-reads it about once a second, so the text changes within one to two seconds. If OBS runs on another PC, use OBS WebSocket.

OBS on another PC, or instant updates: OBS WebSocket (optional)

The app can put the same text straight into a Text source through OBS’s built-in WebSocket server (OBS 28 or newer). Updates show at once, the source needs no file path, and OBS can be on a different PC. The text file is still written either way.

  1. In OBS: Tools > WebSocket Server Settings → tick Enable WebSocket server (port 4455). Leave Enable Authentication on and click Show Connect Info to see the password.
  2. In the app: Overlay page → OBS WebSocket → tick Update a Text source in OBS directly.
  3. OBS on this PC: leave the address as 127.0.0.1. OBS on another PC: enter that PC’s IPv4 address (run ipconfig there) and allow TCP port 4455 in its Windows Firewall, on a Private network.
  4. Paste the password and click Connect.
  5. Pick your Text source in the list. Use one without “Read from file”, or click Use direct updates if it reads the file (the app never changes that setting by itself).
  • The status line says what is happening: Connected, Waiting for OBS (the app keeps trying and catches up when OBS is back), Password problem, and so on.
  • The password is kept in Windows Credential Manager, never in config.toml, a log or the diagnostics report.
  • The app only lists sources, reads one source’s settings and sets that source’s text. It cannot start or stop your stream or change scenes.
  • Keep this on your own local network. OBS’s WebSocket connection is not encrypted, so never forward port 4455 to the internet.
  • Do not let two tools update the same Text source, or the text will flicker. Give each tool its own source.
Less tested: this has been tested against a simulated OBS server, and not yet with OBS on a second PC.

Customize what viewers see

Open the Overlay page in the app:

  • What OBS shows: both numbers on two lines, both on one line, only the estimated total, or only the confirmed chests. Each choice shows an example with your current numbers.
  • Wording: put # where the number goes, e.g. Chests opened: # or Chest Hunt: #/500. For the one-line choice, #C is the confirmed number and #A the estimated total. Original wording brings the default text back.
  • Preview shows exactly what the text file will contain. Click Apply: OBS updates at once, even while counting.

OBS always reads the same file (chest_counter.txt), whatever you choose, so you never change the OBS source. Any text, symbols and emoji work (emoji need an OBS font that has them).

The app cannot hide a Text source you already added in OBS. If you see a number you did not choose, that source reads a different file: point it at obs_output\chest_counter.txt or hide it. The app’s Dashboard always shows both numbers — that is for you, not for viewers.

Advanced: the same settings live in config.toml under [display]. For separately styled sources, the counter also writes:

Dashboard views: Overview, Game and Console

The Dashboard has three views, switched at the top. Overview has your lifetime numbers, the video source, Start counting and a small Live preview. Game shows this match only: its chests, chests per minute and time played, from match tracking, with a larger preview. Console has the Activity log and diagnostic recording; its button shows a number when there are warnings you have not seen. Changing view, or minimising the app, never changes what is counted.

Activity lists what the counter sees and decides, live: green = certain chest · yellow = counted in APPROX only · red = looked like a chest pop-up but was not counted. match 0.94 is how closely the pop-up word matched (0–1), not a percentage or probability. Switch Normal detail to Debug detail to also see every candidate, the full reason for each decision and a status line every few seconds.

Live preview shows the picture the counter is analysing (a few times per second), so you can check that it sees the game full screen with the HUD. It is on by default and can be hidden with its button. Only the one on screen updates, and neither changes what is counted.

Everything is also written to the session log in logs\, whether the panels are open or not.

Help improve accuracy: marks and diagnostic recording (optional)

On the Dashboard’s Console view, Diagnostic recording (off by default) keeps the video the counter analysed, so a missed chest can be replayed and checked later. Change it any time, also while counting — the counts are not affected:

  • Only around my marks — the smallest: the counter keeps a short rolling recording and saves only the 30 seconds before and 10 seconds after each mark.
  • Around chest decisions and my marks — also about 20 seconds around every chest the counter considered.
  • Whole session — large (about 10 GB per hour), for finding chests the counter never noticed.

Mark a missed chest: right after the counter misses a chest (or counts something that was not one), press Ctrl+Alt+M — it works while Fortnite has the focus — or click Mark moment. A green “Marked” line confirms it. Marking needs a recording running (there is no video to keep otherwise), so the button is grey and the hotkey only shows a hint while recording is off. Lossless (Settings) keeps exactly what the counter saw, so a replay reproduces every decision, at about twice the disk space.

Each counting session gets a folder in captures\ with the video, every decision the counter made and a labels.csv you can fill in (chest, not_chest, missed_chest…). Review captures… on the Settings page replays every capture through the current version of the counter and compares it with what was counted live and with your labels; it shows which capture it is on, how much video is done and, once the speed is known, the time left. Recording never slows down or changes the counting; it needs at least 2 GB of free disk space and stops by itself below 1 GB.

Counting a recorded video

The app can also analyse a prerecorded gameplay video (.mp4, .mkv, .mov…). On the Dashboard click Analyse a recording…, choose the file and click Start. It runs faster than real time, shows progress and each counted chest, and finishes with the CERTAIN and APPROX numbers (APPROX includes CERTAIN). This never changes your live counter; the result text files go to video_output\. The video should be clean 16:9 gameplay with the HUD visible, in English or Brazilian Portuguese.

Already opened chests? Set a starting count

On the Dashboard click Edit counts…, enter how many chests you have already opened (for example 150) as Chests opened (APPROX), leave Confirmed chests (CERTAIN) at 0 and click Save.

APPROX starts at your number and new chests are added on top. CERTAIN starts at 0, because the counter did not see the earlier chests. The same window corrects a wrong count. It works while counting: the counter picks up the new numbers within a second.

One chest off? The +1 and −1 buttons next to the numbers change APPROX by one, also while counting. CERTAIN never changes this way, because the counter did not confirm that chest, and APPROX cannot go below CERTAIN. A line then shows how much was counted and how much was adjusted by hand, with Undo and an optional Why?. Fortnite can count a chest with nothing on screen to detect (a broken chest, for example), so a +1 does not have to mean the counter made a mistake. A Reset… or Edit counts… replaces the total, so corrections made before it can no longer be undone; they stay in the history.

Profiles: separate counts per person

Count several streamers (or yourself and others) without mixing their numbers: the Profile row on the Dashboard has New profile…, Rename… and Delete…. Each profile keeps its own counts, its own chest-type split and its own video source (for example that streamer’s Twitch link). One profile counts at a time; switch when counting is stopped. OBS always shows the selected profile, so the OBS text source never changes. Counts from earlier versions are the Default profile. A deleted profile’s counts are kept as a backup in state\profiles\deleted\.

Chest types

The line under the two numbers splits the APPROX total by what the pop-up says: Chest, Rare chest, Sprite chest, or Unknown type when the line could not be read reliably (a chest is never guessed to be a normal one). It is a label only: CERTAIN and APPROX are counted exactly as before, and OBS still shows only the totals. Chests counted before v0.2.2, or set with Edit counts…, are listed as not classified. Chest types are read for English pop-ups.

Match tracking and Analytics (experimental)

New in v0.3.0 and on by default; you can turn it off in Settings > Match tracking (experimental). While counting, the app follows your matches and keeps a history of them, per profile, in state\history\ on your PC. It never changes your counts or what OBS shows.

Save progress every (30 seconds, 1 minute, 5 minutes or 10 minutes) sets how often running totals are written. Matches are followed once a second whatever you choose. A longer interval writes less to the disk, but after a crash more of the last moments count as uncertain.

The Game view counts only chests detected during the current match, starting from zero each match. It shows a dash, not a zero, until the app is sure it is watching a match. Its own +1 and −1 fix this match’s count (between matches, the one that just ended) and your total at the same time, for a chest the counter missed, a broken chest, or something it should not have counted. Such a chest is shown as manual, never as a detected one, and Undo takes it back. Next to +1, say what it was for: choose Broken chest only when you know a chest was broken (Analytics counts those apart, as reported by you); the default, Not sure / balance fix, claims nothing. The +1 / −1 on Overview change only your total.

The Analytics page shows Average detected chests / min: APPROX chests the app detected by itself, divided by your time actively playing, across complete matches only. It needs five minutes of active play across those matches; until then it says Not enough complete matches. Including partial matches is the same sum with partial matches added, shown apart. Recent matches shows each match’s own rate. A −1 made in the Game view always counts in these figures, because it takes off a chest that should not have been counted; a +1 made there counts only if you tick Count my +1s from the Game view, so by default the rates stay what the app detected. Your official totals stay the numbers on the Dashboard. The waiting island before a match is never listed as a match.

QualityMeaning
CompleteIts start and the end of your part in it were both seen in English, with no break in the video. How it ended and how long it lasted do not matter: placed, won, or back to the menus (Left the match, Returned to lobby). Only these enter the main rate and complete-match averages.
PartialThe start or the end was not seen: joined late, counting stopped, or the video was lost. Its time still shows.
UnconfirmedNo English evidence that it was really a match. Left out of every statistic.

If a recorded match was wrong, Not a match leaves it out of the statistics and Restore puts it back. Nothing is deleted, and the chests stay in your totals.

Match recognition reads English screens only for now, and has been tested on only a few real matches. In another language the chests are still counted, but matches show as “not sure” and no statistics are made up.

Reset the counter (e.g. at the start of a stream)

On the Dashboard click Reset… and confirm. Both numbers (and the chest-type split) of the selected profile go to 0, also while counting. Closing and reopening the app does not reset anything; your totals continue.

Settings (optional)

Most people never need to change anything. The Settings page has the Fortnite language (English, Portuguese (Brazil) or Both; pick the one your game is set to), the session logs and the optional diagnostic recording. Changes are used the next time you click Start counting. Updating to a new version

From v0.2.3 on, the app has a built-in updater (experimental). Shortly after opening it checks for a new version and, if there is one, shows a small Update available link in the sidebar. Choose Download & Install: counting keeps running while the update is downloaded and prepared, and Restart & Install finishes it. It is built to keep your counts, profiles, settings and OBS sources, and to return to the previous version by itself if a new one does not start properly. Nothing is downloaded or installed unless you choose to. The automatic check and the download speed are in Settings > Updates.

v0.3.0 is the first release the updater can install, so it is the updater’s first real update. If it does not work, update by hand with the steps below, which work for any version. Versions older than v0.2.3 also need these steps, because they have no updater.

Your counter, settings and logs are stored next to the program files, never inside them:

KeepWhat it is
state\Your saved counts and match history (every profile).
config.tomlYour settings (if any were changed).
logs\Session logs (optional to keep).
obs_output\The text files OBS reads (they are re-created automatically).

To update by hand: close the app, extract the new zip to its own folder, then copy state\ and config.toml from the old folder into the new one. The zip never contains your counts or config.toml. Opening the new app writes your real numbers into its OBS text files (the zip contains blank placeholders). If you use OBS, point your Text source at the new obs_output folder.

Coming from v0.1.x: after extracting, delete the old .bat and .cmd files from the folder — v0.2.0 is one app, Fortnite Visual Chest Counter.exe. Your counts, settings and OBS sources keep working.

Fortnite compatibility

The chest detection in v0.2.2 recognises English chest pop-ups on a normal, larger or smaller HUD, chests added to a pop-up already on screen (“RARE CHEST X2”, another chest line), and Brazilian Portuguese pop-ups on a normal HUD. It was developed and live-tested during Fortnite Chapter 7 Season 5 (November 2026); its automated tests also use recordings of earlier gameplay and real-world cases from user recordings. It recognises Fortnite’s on-screen chest pop-up and HUD elements, which Epic Games can change in any update. If chests stop being counted after a Fortnite update, please report it on the project’s GitHub page (Issues: https://github.com/GabrielGzS/fortnite-visual-chest-counter/issues) with the diagnostics report.

Known limitations (please read)

  • APPROX is an estimate. It can be a little high or low. CERTAIN is the conservative number; APPROX is the best guess of the total.
  • Language: chest detection uses the word in the pop-up. Supported: English (CHEST, incl. RARE CHEST and SPRITE CHEST) and Brazilian Portuguese (BAÚ). Other languages are not supported yet. Chest types are read for English only.
  • HUD layout: it expects the HUD in its usual place at a 16:9 picture. For English pop-ups a HUD drawn larger (up to about 1.3×) or smaller (about 0.8×, tested on one stream) is recognised; those chests count in APPROX only, never CERTAIN. Other HUD sizes, a moved or hidden HUD, Portuguese pop-ups on a non-standard HUD, or a 4:3 / vertical picture will not work well.
  • Chests opened in quick succession are added to the pop-up on screen (“RARE CHEST X2”, “X3”, another chest line). They are counted (multiplier steps in APPROX only); two chests within a fraction of a second can count as one — never as two for one chest.
  • Livestream URL is a lower-confidence convenience option. It sees what viewers get: compression, bursts, buffering, ad breaks and reconnects. It can miss chests, and how many has not been measured. Screen: the gameplay must fill the screen.
  • Clean gameplay feed recommended: overlays, webcam or alerts on top of the HUD can hide the clues.
  • Audio is not used. Only the picture is analysed.
  • Match tracking is experimental: English screens only, tested on recordings and a few real matches. Other modes, reboots and squads are untested. Your counts never depend on it.
  • Tested on a limited number of recordings (PC English, PS5 Portuguese, streams with a larger and a smaller HUD, 11 labelled real-world cases) and live sessions up to several hours. Physical capture cards and real OBS projectors are not tested yet. Other platforms or settings may behave differently — that’s why this is a beta.

Fair play and compatibility

The counter is built for statistics and stream presentation: it counts chest openings that are already visible on screen. It gives no competitive information — it does not locate hidden chests, reveal enemies, aim, move or play for you. It has no connection to Fortnite: it only reads video frames (a livestream, a screen capture, OBS Virtual Camera, a capture device or a video file), and its one keyboard shortcut (Ctrl+Alt+M) is a standard Windows hotkey that sends nothing to the game.

Epic Games’ rules prohibit tools that use real-time gameplay information to gain a competitive advantage. This app is designed for counting, not for competitive assistance — but Epic does not certify individual third-party applications, and this one has not been reviewed or approved by Epic. The most conservative ways to use it: count recorded video (Analyse a recording), or run the counter on a separate streaming PC (two-PC setup), so nothing from this project runs where Fortnite runs. Running it on the same PC as Fortnite works technically; in one developer session the app analysed a livestream on the same PC while Fortnite was being played, and BattlEye did not interrupt the game — one observation, not a guarantee. Its compatibility with Fortnite’s anti-cheat has not been officially verified, and anti-cheat behaviour can change at any time. A two-PC setup is not a compliance guarantee either; it simply keeps the counter away from the game. Tournament or event rules can be stricter — check them if you compete.

Privacy and safety

The app sends nothing anywhere. It connects to the internet only to fetch a public livestream you paste (Livestream URL), the way a video player does; it reads video frames from the source you pick and writes text files and logs in its own folder. The live preview is shown only in the app on your screen; it is never saved or sent. It does not inject into, read memory from or modify Fortnite.
